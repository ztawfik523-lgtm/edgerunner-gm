# Prose editing

Read the full draft before editing. Identify a concrete issue, then make the smallest change that resolves it.

- Preserve facts, names, dates, quantities, attribution, quotations, technical vocabulary, negation, scope, and meaningful uncertainty. Do not strengthen or weaken claims without evidence and authorization.
- Preserve the author's intent and voice. Do not manufacture anecdotes, personality, or certainty to make prose seem more human.
- Look for wording that is vague, inflated, repetitive, formulaic, or detached from the evidence: stock introductions, generic importance claims, repetitive signposts, canned contrasts, list-shaped prose, and filler conclusions.
- Judge each candidate in context. A phrase, punctuation mark, structural pattern, or scanner hit does not establish AI authorship or prove that the passage is defective.
- Prefer deletion when words add no meaning; otherwise use a concrete replacement. Avoid rewriting clean material just to impose a style.
- In an audit, report the passage, issue, and reason; distinguish clear defects from matters of taste. Do not silently rewrite when the user asked only for a review.
- After editing, check that the result preserves the source's claims and reads coherently as a whole.

These principles adapt the contextual editing and preservation ideas in [theclaymethod/unslop](https://github.com/theclaymethod/unslop). Its scanners and benchmark thresholds are not included here; the upstream README reports that its latest independent core benchmark did not meet its stated safety bar.

For drafting or maintaining full documents, use `writing-documents`. For evidence gathering and factual synthesis, use `research-synthesis`.
